Anatomy of the Human Eye

The human eye is an amazing organ that allows us to see the world around us. It is made up of many different parts that work together to collect light, focus images, and send signals to the brain. Each part has an important job that helps us see clearly every day.

Parts of the Human Eye

Cornea

The cornea is the clear covering at the front of the eye. It protects the eye and helps bend light.

Iris

The iris is the coloured part of the eye. It controls how much light enters the eye.

Pupil

The pupil is the black circle in the middle of the iris. It allows light to enter the eye.

Lens

The lens focuses light onto the retina so we can see objects clearly.

Retina

The retina is at the back of the eye. It detects light and changes it into signals for the brain.

Optic Nerve

The optic nerve carries signals from the retina to the brain so we can understand what we see.

Sclera

The sclera is the white outer layer of the eye. It protects the eye and helps it keep its shape.

Vitreous Humor

The vitreous humor is a clear, jelly-like substance that fills the inside of the eye and helps it keep its shape.

Aqueous Humor

The aqueous humor is a clear liquid in the front of the eye. It helps nourish the eye and maintain its pressure.

Choroid

The choroid is a layer with blood vessels that supplies oxygen and nutrients to the eye.

Ciliary Body

The ciliary body helps change the shape of the lens so we can focus on objects near and far away.
