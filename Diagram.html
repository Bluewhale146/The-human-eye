How Do Our Eyes Work?

Our eyes allow us to see the world around us. They collect light and send information to our brain, which helps us understand what we see.

1. The Cornea

The cornea is the clear outer layer of the eye. It protects the eye and bends incoming light.

2. The Iris and Pupil

The iris is the coloured part of the eye. It controls the size of the pupil, which lets light enter.

3. The Lens

The lens focuses light onto the retina so that we can see objects clearly.

4. The Retina

The retina detects light and changes it into electrical signals.

5. The Optic Nerve

The optic nerve carries signals to the brain, allowing us to understand what we see.

What Can Affect Our Eyes?

Many things can affect our eyesight and the health of our eyes. Some can cause temporary irritation, while others can cause long-term damage.

Screen Time

Looking at screens for too long can cause tired eyes, dryness and headaches.

Sunlight

Too much UV light from the sun can damage the eyes over time.

Infections

Bacteria and viruses can cause redness, swelling and eye irritation.

Eye Injuries

Accidents, chemicals and dust can hurt the eye and affect vision.

Ageing

As people get older, their eyesight can change and they may develop cataracts.

Genetics

Some eye conditions can be passed down through families.
